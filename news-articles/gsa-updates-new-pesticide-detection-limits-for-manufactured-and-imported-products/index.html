Ghana Standards Authority Updates New Pesticide Detection Limits for Manufactured and Imported Products

Kofi Mensah

Agriculture Editor

October 15, 2022 4 min read 10,250 views

The Ghana Standards Authority (GSA) has announced a significant update to its pesticide residue detection limits for manufactured and imported agricultural products, citing the advancement as a groundbreaking achievement for the nation's agricultural quality control and public health. This milestone was made possible through a collaborative research effort led by the Quality Control Company Limited (QCC) of the Ghana Cocoa Board (COCOBOD).

The research focused on developing robust methods to detect and quantify pesticide residues, including Acetamiprid and Chlorpyrifos, using cutting-edge analytical techniques like Gas Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (GC-MS) and Liquid Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (LC-MS/MS). The findings were instrumental in defining new Limits of Detection (LOD) and Limits of Quantification (LOQ), which form the foundation of the updated standards. The updated limits have now been formally adopted by the Ghana Standards Authority as the benchmark for ensuring compliance with both local and international safety regulations. These standards will apply to a wide range of agricultural products, including over 500 local and imported items such as cocoa, coffee, maize, vegetables, and processed food products.

Dr. Opoku Agyeman, Director General of the Ghana Standards Authority, commended the dedication and expertise demonstrated by COCOBOD’s Quality Control Company throughout the research process.

"The new standards represent a major step forward in safeguarding public health and ensuring that Ghana's agricultural products remain competitive on the global market. This achievement would not have been possible without the hard work and technical expertise of COCOBOD's Quality Control team," Dr. Agyeman stated. "Their pioneering work has set a new benchmark for pesticide residue monitoring, providing Ghana with the tools to meet stringent international standards and enhance the safety of our food systems.

Our work aimed to improve sensitivity and accuracy in detecting harmful pesticide residues, ensuring compliance with international trade requirements and protecting consumer health," said Dr. Cox Banahene - Snr Research Officer at COCOBOD. "It is an honor to see our efforts contribute to the establishment of these updated standards by the GSA."

The updated detection limits now apply to key agricultural products including cocoa beans (Product Code: AGC-2024), fresh vegetables (Product Code: VEG-5001), and processed maize flour (Product Code: MAI-3020). These revisions will also impact imported food items, ensuring that they meet the same rigorous safety standards.
